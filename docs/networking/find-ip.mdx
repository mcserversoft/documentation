---
title: "Find the right address"
description: "Learn when to use localhost, your local network (LAN) IP, or your public IP address."
---

The IP address a player uses to join your server depends on where they are connecting from. The examples below use the default Minecraft Java Edition port (`25565`)—if your server uses a different port, replace `25565` with your server's port.

| Who is connecting? | Address to use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **You**, playing on the same PC running MCSS | `localhost` or `127.0.0.1` | `localhost:25565` |
| **Someone in your home**, on the same Wi-Fi or wired network (LAN) | The server PC's **LAN IPv4 address** | `192.168.1.50:25565` |
| **Friends over the internet** (with [port forwarding](/networking/port-forwarding)) | Your router's **public IPv4 address** or domain | `203.0.113.10:25565` |
| **Anyone** (when using a tunnel like [playit.gg](/advanced/using-playit.gg-with-mcss)) | The **tunnel address** shown in your tunnel dashboard | `example.gl.joinmc.link` |

> [!TIP]
> `localhost` always means *"this computer right here."* Use it when your Minecraft game and MCSS are running on the exact same PC. Anyone connecting from another computer or console needs your LAN IP or public IP instead.

## Find your LAN address on Windows

Players on your home network need your server computer's local (LAN) IPv4 address. You will also need this address when setting up [port forwarding](/networking/port-forwarding).

1. Press **Win + R**, type `cmd`, and press **Enter** to open Command Prompt.
2. Run the following command:

```cmd
ipconfig
```

3. Look for the network adapter your computer uses to connect to your router (usually **Ethernet adapter Ethernet** or **Wireless LAN adapter Wi-Fi**). Ignore virtual adapters from VPNs or virtual machines.
4. Note the **IPv4 Address** and **Default Gateway** lines:

```text
IPv4 Address. . . . . . . . . . . : 192.168.1.50
Subnet Mask . . . . . . . . . . . : 255.255.255.0
Default Gateway . . . . . . . . . : 192.168.1.1
```

- **IPv4 Address (`192.168.1.50` in this example):** This is your server computer's LAN address. Other computers at home can connect using `192.168.1.50:25565`.
- **Default Gateway (`192.168.1.1` in this example):** This is your router's address, which you use to open your router's settings page.

> [!NOTE]
> Because home routers assign LAN addresses automatically using DHCP, your computer's LAN IP can occasionally change after a reboot. Most routers let you set a **DHCP reservation** (or **Static Lease**) in their LAN settings so your server PC always keeps the same local IP address.

## Find your public IPv4 address

Friends connecting from outside your home cannot use your `192.168.x.x` or `10.x.x.x` LAN address—those addresses only work inside your local network. Instead, they need your **public IPv4 address**.

To find your public IPv4 address:

1. On the server computer (with any VPN turned off), visit an IP lookup page such as [What Is My IP Address](https://whatismyipaddress.com/).
2. Copy the **IPv4** address displayed on the page.

Before friends can join using your public IPv4 address, you must [set up a port forwarding rule](/networking/port-forwarding) on your router so incoming game traffic knows which computer in your home to reach.

## IPv6 addresses

When you look up your IP address, you may also see a long **IPv6 address** made of letters, numbers, and colons (for example, `2001:db8:85a3::8a2e:370:7334`).

- **Using IPv4 is usually easiest:** Most Minecraft server setups and home routers use IPv4 for port forwarding. If you have a public IPv4 address, share that with your players.
- **Formatting IPv6 in Minecraft:** If both you and your players have IPv6 internet connectivity and you want to connect over IPv6, wrap the IPv6 address in square brackets when adding a port in Minecraft Java Edition:
  ```text
  [2001:db8:85a3::8a2e:370:7334]:25565
  ```
- **Ignore `fe80:` addresses:** Any IPv6 address starting with `fe80:` is a *link-local* address that only works on your immediate local network, not over the internet.
